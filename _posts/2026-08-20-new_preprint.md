In this study, Kesem and Shir introduce a framework for using contextual evidence to categorise ecological interactions and guide the discovery of unknown ones. By leveraging structural and contextual features of interaction networks, the approach offers a principled way to distinguish between different types of ecological relationships and to prioritise which missing interactions are most worth investigating in the field. This work advances the toolkit for ecologists working on large, incompletely sampled interaction networks.

Read the full paper here: [EcoEvoRxiv](https://doi.org/hcgk8v)
